2.5 Steps in the Data Science Process

Every data science project follows five steps: Acquire, Prepare, Analyze, Report, Act, and then loops back with new questions. (lao, saaf karo, samjho, batao, karo)

Definition: The data science process is an iterative sequence of five steps: Acquire data, Prepare it (explore + pre-process), Analyze it (build and evaluate models), Report the insights, and Act on them.
  • 1. Acquire: identify suitable data sets and retrieve / query them. Four main source categories: traditional databases (SQL), text files (CSV, logs), remote data (web services / APIs), NoSQL storage. (data lao)
  • 2. Prepare has two parts:
    • Explore: understand the nature of data, preliminary analysis: summary statistics (mean, median, range), correlations, trends, outliers, plots.
    • Pre-process: clean (missing, invalid, duplicate values, outliers) and wrangle / munge (scaling, transformation, feature selection, dimensionality reduction, data manipulation such as merging).
  • 3. Analyze: select technique → build model → evaluate. Techniques: classification, regression, clustering, association analysis, graph analytics.
  • 4. Report: communicate results with visualisations; state main findings, their value and limits, to non-technical decision makers.
  • 5. Act: turn insight into action (deploy model, change business process), monitor results; new questions start the loop again.

Example (IPL team picking bowlers)
Acquire: ball-by-ball data of past seasons from files and a stats API.
Prepare: explore economy rates; remove rain-shortened duplicates, fill missing venue names.
Analyze: regression to predict runs conceded per over at each venue; evaluate on last season.
Report: chart of best death-over bowlers by venue for the coach.
Act: choose bowlers per ground; check results after matches and refine.

An alternative framing: the 6-stage cycle (CRISP-DM)

Many sources (and a well-known data-mining standard, CRISP-DM) draw the same idea as a 6-stage circle instead of 5 boxes in a row, by splitting Prepare and Analyze each into two.

  • Business / problem understanding = part of our Acquire: fixing the goal before touching data.
  • Data understanding = the "Explore" half of our Prepare step.
  • Data preparation = the "Pre-process" half of our Prepare step (clean + wrangle).
  • Modeling and Evaluation = our Analyze step, split into building and testing.
  • Deployment = our Act step (put the model to work, then monitor it).
Mnemonic "All Players Are Really Active" = Acquire, Prepare, Analyze, Report, Act. Prepare = Explore + Pre-process ("EP").
Common mistake: treating the steps as one-way. The process is iterative; Report / Act often send you back to Acquire or Prepare. Also, the 5-step (Acquire-Prepare-Analyze-Report-Act) and 6-stage (CRISP-DM) versions are the SAME underlying process at different granularity, not two processes to memorise separately — use whichever the question names.
Quick recall A-P-A-R-A; Prepare = explore + pre-process; Analyze = select technique, build model, evaluate. Same idea, 6-stage version: Business understanding → Data understanding → Data preparation → Modeling → Evaluation → Deployment.
