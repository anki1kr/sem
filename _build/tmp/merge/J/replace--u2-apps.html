2.12 Real Big Data Management Applications

Real companies face all six management concerns at once: an energy utility with smart meters and a game-analytics company with millions of player events. (asli duniya mein sab ek saath)

Definition: A big data management application is a real system in which an organisation ingests, stores, cleans, processes, scales and secures large, fast, varied data to support its operations and decisions.
  • Case studies used in the UCSD course: energy sector (ConEd, a New York utility), gaming industry (Apmetrix, game analytics), and flight data (FlightStats).
  • Energy utility:
    • Ingestion: continuous readings from a very large number of smart meters and grid sensors (high velocity).
    • Storage: time-series style data that keeps growing, so scale-out storage.
    • Quality: missing or faulty meter readings must be detected and estimated for correct bills.
    • Operations: aggregation of usage by area and time to forecast demand and detect outages.
    • Security/privacy: household usage reveals personal routines, so access control and encryption are needed.
  • Gaming analytics:
    • Ingestion: every player action (level start, purchase, quit) is an event from many devices and platforms (variety + velocity).
    • Scalability: player numbers spike at launch or weekends, so storage and processing must scale out elastically.
    • Operations: filter and aggregate events to measure retention, session length, in-game purchases.
    • Action: change difficulty, personalise offers, fix levels where players quit.
    • Security: player accounts and payment data must be protected.
  • Flight data: flight status data from many airlines and airports must be integrated in near real time, cleaned of conflicting reports, and served quickly to apps and travellers.

Real-World Big Data Applications by Sector

  • Healthcare: patient-data analysis for better diagnosis, predictive analytics for disease outbreaks, AI-based medical image analysis.
  • Finance: fraud detection in transactions, algorithmic trading, risk management and credit scoring.
  • E-commerce and retail: recommendation systems, predicting customer behaviour for marketing, inventory and supply-chain optimisation.
  • Social media: sentiment analysis of posts, trend detection (viral content, hashtags), targeted advertising from user activity.
  • Smart cities and IoT: traffic-flow monitoring, energy-consumption management, predictive maintenance of public infrastructure.
