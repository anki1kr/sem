Why does HDFS split a file into blocks?
Storing and seeking through one huge file on a single disk is slow; splitting it into fixed-size blocks (128 MB) lets many machines store and read/write parts in parallel, cutting seek time.
What are the fsimage and the edit log?
The fsimage is a snapshot of the NameNode's metadata at one point in time; the edit log records every change since. On restart the NameNode reloads the fsimage and replays the edit log to rebuild its current state.
List the four features of YARN.
Multi-tenancy (many engines share one cluster), Scalability, Cluster-utilisation (dynamic resource sharing), Compatibility (old MapReduce jobs still run).
What do RecordReader and OutputFormat do in MapReduce?
RecordReader converts an input split into (key, value) records for the Map function; OutputFormat (via a RecordWriter) writes the Reducer's final (key, value) pairs to HDFS, one record per line.
Is it correct to say NoSQL databases have no ACID support at all?
No, that is an oversimplification. Aggregate-oriented NoSQL databases are atomic within a single aggregate, and some products (e.g. MongoDB since v4.0) support multi-document ACID transactions. What NoSQL generally lacks is cheap ACID transactions spanning many aggregates/rows at once, unlike an RDBMS.
